37 Moments Across 99 Years

Every milestone entered as a Moment in My Tempus — with photos, moods, tags, and location. Five views to explore them.

—— Youth & War ——
1924

Born in Omaha, Nebraska

Charles Thomas Munger is born on January 1, 1924, to Alfred and Florence Munger. His grandfather was a federal judge; his father was a lawyer and later a Nebraska state senator.

📍 Omaha, NE🧑‍🍼#birth
1941

Enters University of Michigan

Studies mathematics at the University of Michigan. Pearl Harbor changes everything — he leaves to serve in World War II.

📍 Ann Arbor, MI#education
1943

U.S. Army Air Corps Meteorologist

Serves as a meteorologist in the Army Air Corps during WWII. The Army sends him to Caltech for meteorological training — his first taste of the West Coast.

📍 Pasadena, CA😤#military #WWII
1946

Harvard Law School — Magna Cum Laude

Accepts a place at Harvard Law School without an undergraduate degree. Graduates in 1948 with a Juris Doctor, magna cum laude. Harvard's toughness shapes his intellectual rigor.

📍 Cambridge, MA🤩#education #law
—— The Lawyer ——
1948

Moves to Los Angeles — Practices Law

Joins the firm Wright & Garrett in Los Angeles. Marries Nancy Huggins. Begins building a reputation as a brilliant, strategic lawyer — one who thinks more like a dealmaker than a litigator.

📍 Los Angeles, CA😊#career
1956

Meets Nancy Borthwick

After his divorce, Munger meets Nancy Borthwick. They marry and build a blended family of 9 children (his 3, her 2, and four more together). His commitment to family becomes a lifelong anchor.

❤️#family
1959

Dinner That Changed Everything — Meets Warren Buffett

At a dinner in Omaha, the 35-year-old Munger meets 29-year-old Warren Buffett. They talk for hours and immediately recognize a kindred mind. "We were on the same wavelength immediately." This dinner plants the seed for one of history's greatest financial partnerships.

📍 Omaha, NE🤩#legendary #berkshire #pivotal
1962

Co-Founds Munger, Tolles & Olson

Establishes one of Los Angeles's most prestigious law firms, Munger, Tolles & Olson. The firm's culture of intellectual independence and meticulous reasoning reflects Munger's own approach to every problem.

📍 Los Angeles, CA#career #law
—— The Investor ——
1962

Launches Investment Partnership

Simultaneously with the law firm, Munger starts an investment partnership — directly paralleling Buffett's partnership in the 1950s. He begins deploying his version of value investing with a twist: willing to pay fair prices for wonderful businesses.

📍 Los Angeles, CA😌#investing
1972

See's Candies — The Paradigm Shift

The single most important investment decision of his and Buffett's lives. Munger convinces Buffett to pay $25 million for See's Candies — 3× book value — violating Graham's "cigar butt" principle. The $40M earned from See's over decades funds Berkshire's entire subsequent empire. Munger calls it "the paradigm shift" that taught them the power of a moat.

📍 San Francisco, CA🤩#berkshire #pivotal #investing
1975

Joins Berkshire Hathaway — Vice Chairman

Munger formally joins Berkshire Hathaway as Vice Chairman. His role is not operational — he is Buffett's sounding board, strategic foil, and intellectual conscience. "Charlie can't be beat at seeing what's wrong with an idea before you even finish describing it."

📍 Omaha, NE😊#berkshire #career
1977

Chairman of Blue Chip Stamps

Takes the helm of Blue Chip Stamps, a Berkshire-controlled company. Demonstrates his ability to manage and restructure businesses — not just evaluate them from the outside.

#berkshire #management
1984

Chairman of Wesco Financial

Becomes Chairman of Wesco Financial, a Berkshire subsidiary. Munger runs annual meetings so idiosyncratic and insightful that Wesco's become a must-attend for value investors until its merger into Berkshire in 2011.

📍 Pasadena, CA😌#berkshire #investing
1986

Wesco Annual Meeting — "The Psychology of Human Misjudgment"

Delivers his first formal talk on cognitive biases at the Wesco annual meeting — the embryonic version of what would become his legendary "Psychology of Human Misjudgment." This becomes his signature intellectual contribution beyond investing.

📍 Pasadena, CA🤩#psychology #philosophy #pivotal
—— The Sage ——
1990

Chairman of Daily Journal Corporation

Takes the Chairmanship of Daily Journal Corp, a legal publishing and software company. He leads it with characteristic independence — buying securities during the 2008 crisis that would later deliver enormous returns.

📍 Los Angeles, CA#media #investing
1997

Joins Costco Board of Directors

Joins the board of Costco Wholesale. Serves alongside founder Jim Sinegal and later CEO Craig Jelinek. Munger's emphasis on culture, efficiency, and long-term thinking finds a perfect match in Costco's operating philosophy. He serves for 25+ years.

📍 Issaquah, WA😊#board #retail
2005

Poor Charlie's Almanack Published

Peter Kaufman compiles Munger's speeches and writings into "Poor Charlie's Almanack." The book — named consciously after Ben Franklin's Poor Richard's Almanack — distills Munger's worldly wisdom on mental models, psychology, investing, and life. It becomes a cult classic and later a mainstream bestseller, especially among a new generation of tech investors.

📖#philosophy #book #pivotal
2008

The BYD Investment — "Munger's China Bet"

Munger personally champions a $230M investment in BYD, a Chinese battery and auto company, through Berkshire subsidiary MidAmerican Energy. It's his deal from start to finish. 15 years later, the stake is worth over $8 billion — one of Berkshire's most successful international investments and a vindication of his independent thinking.

📍 Shenzhen, China🤩#berkshire #BYD #investing #pivotal
2010

Defined as "The Complete Investor"

Tren Griffin publishes "Charlie Munger: The Complete Investor," a systematic analysis of Munger's investment framework. The book crystallizes his philosophy for a generation of value investors and helps spread his ideas beyond Berkshire's orbit.

📖#investing #philosophy
—— The Final Chapter ——
2014

90th Birthday — Still at the Table

Turns 90 with his mind as sharp as ever. Still attending every Berkshire board meeting and Daily Journal shareholder meeting. The 2014 Daily Journal meeting includes one of his most-quoted lines: "If you keep your life on track, the inevitable difficulties that come to everybody will be much easier to bear."

😊#milestone
2020

Steps Down as Daily Journal Chairman

At age 96, steps down as Chairman of Daily Journal Corp after 30 years at the helm. Even in retirement, his investment decisions for the Journal's portfolio continue to be studied by value investors worldwide.

😌#retirement
2022

Last Public Interview — CNBC

Gives his last major interview to CNBC's Becky Quick. At 98, his mind remains sharp and contrarian. He discusses BYD, Costco, Berkshire, and the future of investing — with characteristic directness and no filter.

🤩#media
2023

Passes at 99 — A Life Complete

Charlie Munger dies peacefully on November 28, 2023, at Santa Barbara Cottage Hospital, just 34 days short of his 100th birthday. His 99-year life — spanning the Great Depression, WWII, the Cold War, the rise of Silicon Valley, and the AI era — is marked by an extraordinary combination of intellectual rigor, moral clarity, and a legendary partnership that reshaped investing forever.

📍 Santa Barbara, CA😢#berkshire #legacy
